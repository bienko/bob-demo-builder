    BOB DEMO-BUILDER MODE

    DEPLOYING WITH DEMO BUILDER

    i. From concept to demo

    Before continuing, a brief recap on where things stand and the work you've carried out so far:

    PRE-REQUISITES

    1. The Marketplace has been installed

    2. All necessary credentials gathered

    3. The MCP servers configured and verified

    Now it's time to put this preparation into practice. In the chapter ahead, you will take a one-sentence use case— an AI agent that watches factory equipment and heads off failures before they occur —and carry it through the Demo Builder's full lifecycle:

    TO-DO'S

    1. Guided intake

    2. Autonomous build

    3. Local launch

    4. Review-and-refine pass

    5. Client-ready demo script

    The flow documented could be described as a "simplified" example and it's been intentionally designed as such to minimize the complexity of your first hands-on experience with this process. It also keeps the Bob tokens consumption to a minimum. For those wanting a more elaborate and resource-intensive example, you will have the opportunity to practice this in the optional chapter ahead.

    For now, with the simplified example this chapter, you will build in mock mode. This approach requires zero credentials and works fully offline — no watsonx.ai environment, no API keys, or anything beyond the workspace you configured in the previous chapter. The purpose is to experience the complete arc of the Demo Builder methodology end-to-end, and to establish repeatable patterns that you can apply (with fuller briefs and real environments) to potential client opportunities at a later time.

    ii. Why this matters

    Take note of the division of labor as you work through this chapter, because it is the very discipline this course has been reinforcing from the beginning: Bob does the building; the human-in-the-loop (you) makes the final decisions and calls the shots. The intake questions that the Demo Builder poses at the start— the build approach, environment, deployment target, scenario, and so on —are critical decision points that you must answer. Once those decisions have been recorded and sanctioned, Bob executes those instructions autonomously: generating, verifying, testing, and (as you will witness first-hand) catching and repairing its own bugs. Throughout this process, you maintain your role as the principle arbiter for approving additional actions, validating the results, and advancing (or re-orienting) the workflow.

    There is a second lesson embedded here as well. Despite "mock mode" having no live models or real environments backing it, the finalized demonstration is a complete, navigable, Carbon-styled application with preset AI scenarios, a results view, an architecture view, and a generated walkthrough script. For engagements where you are short on time (or short on Bob tokens before the month rolls over), these types of lightweight demonstrations can be immensely valuable in a pinch.

    A WORD ON WORKING WITH GENERATIVE AI

    IBM Bob is powered by generative AI and LLMs, and a defining trait of these systems is that they are non-deterministic — unlike the deterministic tools most developers are accustomed to. In practice, that means the same prompt can produce different code from one run to the next. This is both a strength and a quirk of the technology, and it is something to work with rather than against.

    Precision is what tips the odds in your favor. The more clearly you describe what you want, the more closely Bob's output will mirror your intent; conversely, the smallest change in wording can meaningfully change the generated code. Throughout this lab, your results may differ from the solutions shown — and that is expected, not a defect. Whenever an output diverges, you have three good options: refine the prompt and try again, edit the generated code by hand, or simply proceed if the variation still compiles and runs. Human review remains essential at every step, and never more so than while you are still learning the tool.

    WANT TO GO DEEPER?

    As prefaced earlier, there is an optional "advanced" form of this workflow that is also documented within the same module. Those of you wishing to go hands-on with deploying real-world environments and live services will greatly enjoy the materials. However, due to the potential of significant Bob token consumption and limitations on the number of environments IBM Technology Zone can allocate to IBM sellers at any one time, the "advanced" section is optional material not required for accreditation of this Level 3 courseware.

    iii. Briefing the builder

    1. Within the Agentic Sidebar, click the Modes selector (which is likely displaying Agent mode at this time) to expose more options:

    1. From the drop-down list, switch to the IBM Pre-Sales Demo Builder mode.

    1. With IBM Bob now in IBM Pre-Sales Demo Builder mode, paste the following prompt into the Agentic Sidebar and press Enter to execute.

      I need a demo of an AI agent that monitors IoT sensor data from factory equipment, predicts failures before they happen, and automatically creates maintenance work orders with recommended parts and technicians.

    1. IBM Pre-Sales Demo Builder mode will first run pre-flight checks to ensure everything is prepared and then begins the intake process.

      • Approve the MCP tool prompt
      • Click the Approve for task toggle to reduce the amount of manual approvals required later in the flow

    1. Once the intake questionnaire is completed, the Pre-Sales Demo Builder mode will present an additional, short sequence of questions (five in the documented example) in order to further tailor the demo to your requirements.

      First, it asks "How should the AI agent be built? [...]". Select the top option:

      watsonx.ai (RECOMMENDED) — A LangGraph ReAct agent running in a FastAPI backend.

      Recall that generative AI systems are non-deterministic by design. Your results may vary.

    1. Next, Pre-Sales Demo Builder mode asks whether you already have a watsonx.ai environment provisioned.

      Select: "No — build in mock mode only (zero credentials needed, works fully offline)"

      In a future section, you will explore how this mode operates when an environment actually is provisioned. For now, mock mode presents the complete overview of the capabilities — with none of the credential overhead.

    1. Next, you are asked "How do you want to run and deploy this demo".

      Select the "Run locally" option that fits your specific operating system — typically one of the top two responses. For example:

      Run locally first — I'm on Intel/AMD (amd64) Mac, Linux, or Windows

    1. Next, it asks "What specific factory equipment scenario should be showcased?"

      Click the top option:

      Industrial CNC machines + conveyor belts + HVAC systems \[...\]

      Here you have more flexibility. If a listed industry or scenario aligns better with your own experience or interests, you are welcome to select it. However, in practice your results may drift further from the captures shown in this guide should you opt to do so.

    1. Finally, Pre-Sales Demo Builder mode requests a final confirmation that everything is in order.

      Select the following option to conclude the fact-gathering phase and move ahead with the build instructions:

      No — we have everything, start building now!

    iv. The autonomous build

    With the intake complete, the balance of the work shifts to Bob. Your role across the following encompasses oversight and steering: review what Bob proposes and either approve the workflow or course-correct as necessary.

    1. Bob begins working. As usual, approve tasks when prompted (if you agree with Bob's recommended course of action.)

    1. Bob creates subdirectories that will store the project.

      • Click "Approve for task"
      • Continue clicking through Approve once / Approve for task as prompted, until the back-end build has concluded

    1. At last, the back-end build has completed and IBM Bob's Pre-Sales Demo Builder mode is now running the build verification.

    1. Pre-Sales Demo Builder mode now runs the back-end tests.

      Click Approve for task once again.

      TROUBLESHOOTING

      Should the Agentic Sidebar freeze or time out at any point, simply sending the prompt continue should everything back on track.

      In this instance, after the continue prompt was sent, Bob understood exactly where it left off and resumed working intelligently. Continue clicking through Approve for task.

    1. Once the back-end deploy is ready, Bob will moves on towards building the front-end.

      Continue clicking Approve for task to advance the workflow.

    1. The build is nearly finished. IBM Pre-Sales Demo Builder mode now wants to run its final compliance tests.

      Continue clicking Approve for task to advance the workflow.

      TROUBLESHOOTING

      1. In this instance, Pre-Sales Demo Builder caught a bug during its compliance pass and begins investigating.

        Click Approve for task.

      2. IBM Pre-Sales Demo Builder mode attempts a different search method to locate the bug.

        Click Approve for task.

      3. Click "Approve for task".

    v. Launch the demo

    1. At this stage, the demo build has been completed and verified.

      Read through the final completion message and locate the Terminal commands (documented within the console read-out) to start the application.

      OPTIONAL: A ONE-CLICK QUICKSTART SCRIPT

      Remember that IBM Bob is remarkably flexible. If something is unclear to you at any point in the process, simply explain it to Bob in natural language.

      A case in point: if you prefer not to open Terminal windows every time you run the app, you can request that Bob creates a quickstart script. A prompt like the following will work:

      Make a quickstart script that allows me to start the app in 1 click, no manual Terminal windows.

      Bob creates the script. Open the File Explorer on your desktop and navigate to the project folder.

      Because the captured example runs on Windows, Bob appropriately produced a batch script START_DEMO.bat that starts the app in one click.

    1. Launch a new Terminal within the Bob IDE by clicking Terminal > New Terminal from the top-line menu bar.

    1. Copy the Terminal 1 - Backend command line string (from Step 16) to your clipboard, paste it into the new Terminal console, and hit Enter to execute.

      Execute these instructions line by line, one at a time.

      Continue executing each instruction from Terminal 1 - Backend until the service has successfully started.

    1. With the back-end service successfully started, you will need to open a second Terminal console to run the front-end service.

      As before, click Terminal > New Terminal from the top-line menu of the Bob IDE. (Alternatively, click the + button to open a new Terminal window.)

    1. Copy the Terminal 2 - Frontend command line string (from Step 16) to your clipboard, paste it into the new Terminal console, and hit Enter to execute.

      Execute these instructions line by line, one at a time.

      Continue executing each instruction from Terminal 2 - Frontend until the service has successfully started.

    1. To finish starting up the app, the final instruction to be executed within the Terminal console should resemble something like the following:

      npm run dev

    1. If successful, the Terminal console should report back that the app has started.

      Hold Ctrl and then left-click the URL to open the address embedded within the Terminal console to view the application with your default web browser.

    vi. Explore and refine

    1. Once the application has loaded within the web browser, begin investigating the various components and features to verify that everything works as expected.

      Keep in mind that your page may look somewhat (or very) different from the app documented here, owing to generative AI's non-deterministic properties.

    1. What happens when you press Run AI Agent? Open the tab to investigate.

    1. In this instance, the AI Analysis executes for the CRITICAL Bearing Failure Alert scenario.

      Proceed to the Results tab.

    1. Everything looks in order here. Continue by clicking Architecture.

    1. Should you experience issues with your demo environment (or you simply wish to personalize it with aesthetic changes), the Agentic Sidebar makes this process relatively straightforward.

      For example, in the demo captured throughout this documentation, the "Fleet Health Index" semicircle needed adjustment. Simply take a screenshot of your application as it is rendered within the web browser, paste that screenshot into the Agentic Sidebar as a reference for comparison, and then request the changes via a natural language prompt that describes the issue.

    1. As requested, the "Fleet Health Index" semicircle now renders properly.

      Whether you are looking to implement new features, make aesthetic changes, debug problematic areas, or something else entirely — the same pattern applies to correcting and enhancing your demo. Take a screenshot of the issue, provide a brief description to IBM Bob in the Agentic Sidebar (in IBM Pre-Sales Demo Builder mode), and Bob will endeavor to implement those changes.

    vii. Pitching the demo script

    1. To conclude the simplified flow demonstration, one task remains: how do you actually work with (and pitch) this demo for a potential client audience?

      Back within the IBM Bob IDE, take note of the Markdown file named DEMO_SCRIPT.md (or something similar) under the Explorer tab. It contains a set of pre-flight checks, alongside the actual demo script you could adapt for walking a prospective client end-to-end through this demonstration. Of course, you can also use the Agentic Sidebar at any time to request for Bob to generate a presentation script for you, bulleted and timed for exactly the length of time you'll have for your client engagement.

      Experiment with this capability now and consider using it to script your next client engagement, Stand & Deliver recording, or tradeshow presentation.

    viii. Key takeaways

    • The simplified flow carries a one-sentence use case through the Demo Builder's complete lifecycle: brief → intake → build → launch → refine → present
    • The guided intake questionnaire covers the essential decision points that you author, and Bob actions: build approach, environment, deployment, scenario, and so on
    • Mock mode produces a complete, navigable, client-credible demonstration with zero credentials and a fully offline environment
    • If the Agentic Sidebar freezes or times out, the continue prompt reliably resumes your work where it left off
    • The screenshot + natural-language prompt pattern is a powerful remedy for debugging and refining your Demo Builder application
    • Leverage DEMO_SCRIPT.md for turning your application into a scripted presentation (or ask Bob to generate one for you)


    In the chapter ahead, you will learn to author the demo brief yourself according to best practices (the six things every prompt must answer) to produce demos that are expertly tailored to a specific client, industry, and audience.
